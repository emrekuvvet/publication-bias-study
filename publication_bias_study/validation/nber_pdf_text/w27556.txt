ABSTRACT
We study the problem of learning about the effect of one market-level variable (e.g., price) on
another (e.g., quantity) in the presence of shocks to unobservables (e.g., preferences). We show
that economic intuitions about the plausible size of the shocks can be informative about the
parameter of interest. We illustrate with a main application to the grain market.
