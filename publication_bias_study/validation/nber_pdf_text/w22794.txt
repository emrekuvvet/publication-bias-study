ABSTRACT
We study optimal policy in an economy in which public debt is used as collateral or liquidity
buffer. Issuing more public debt raises welfare by easing the underlying financial friction; but this
easing lowers the liquidity premium and increases the government’s cost of borrowing. These
considerations, which are absent in the basic Ramsey paradigm, help pin down a unique, long-run
level of public debt. They require a front-loaded tax response to government-spending shocks,
instead of tax smoothing. And they explain why a financial recession, more than a traditional one,
makes government borrowing cheaper, optimally supporting larger fiscal stimuli.
